# NOTES — variant vB

"Sold the house. Kept the ghost."

Against the judge criteria: (1) it is a complete story in six words — a
sale, a move, and a haunting, with a beginning (sold), a middle (the house
changes hands), and an ending (something was kept). (2) Every word earns its
place — the parallel verbs "Sold" and "Kept" set up a transaction, "the house"
is what was expected to be the whole story, and "the ghost" replaces it as
the thing that actually mattered. (3) The turn is the surprise: the reader
expects the second sentence to name something ordinary kept from the house —
the piano, the photographs — and instead it is the ghost, which reframes the
entire first sentence: the house was never the point. (4) It leaves room for
the reader: who is the ghost, did it follow them to the new place, or did
they stay behind with it? The line ends on a mystery it refuses to solve.
